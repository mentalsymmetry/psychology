# The Theory of Mental Symmetry

## What Is Mental Symmetry?

Mental symmetry is a cognitive metatheory, which means that it compares written material in different contexts for similar patterns of cognition and behavior. The premise is that any similarities are the result of mental architecture and not the subject matter being studied. This is not standard academic methodology, which applies rigorous thinking within some specialization, but it is very effective — especially when comparing wildly different fields. A field being analyzed does not have to be empirically provable as long as the written material attempts to honestly describe the field.

## Origins

The theory of mental symmetry originated as a system of cognitive styles that divides people into seven different categories. This system of cognitive styles was first developed in the 1970s and was expanded by Lane Friesen in the 1980s through an analysis of approximately 200 biographies (Friesen, 1986).

This system of cognitive styles turned into a cognitive model when it was realized that the fundamental traits of each cognitive style correspond to the general functioning of a specific brain region. Since everyone has a complete brain, this led to the conclusion that cognitive style relates in some way to consciousness: **everyone has all seven cognitive modules, but cognitive style is determined by the cognitive module in which a person is conscious** — consciousness being defined operationally as awareness and control. Thus, one can speak either of cognitive styles or of cognitive modules. The explaining power of mental symmetry emerges when one thinks in terms of cognitive modules.

## The Diagram of Mental Symmetry

The theory of mental symmetry is summarized by a simple diagram known as the **diagram of mental symmetry**. The seven cognitive modules are each given a label that describes a major cognitive trait: Teacher, Exhorter, Mercy, Server, Contributor, Perceiver, and Facilitator. Studying personality has revealed details that are not explicitly shown by the diagram of mental symmetry, but this diagram still remains the best way to summarize the traits in a simple manner, and the diagram has been arranged in a manner that maps in a general way onto neurology. Thus, the seven labels (Teacher, Exhorter, Mercy, etc.) in the diagram of mental symmetry should be viewed as large-scale cognitive modules each composed of several interacting parts. The term *Mercy thought* will be used to refer to a cognitive module, while the term *Mercy person* refers to a cognitive style.

### Two Primary Axes

The diagram uses two primary axes:

1. **Analytical vs. Associative** — corresponds roughly to left hemisphere vs. right hemisphere. Analytical processing focuses upon sequences, time, order, and structure, while associative processing emphasizes objects, space, connections, and specifics. While these two mental strategies are quite different, most tasks can be handled using either — or both — of these two strategies.

2. **Emotion vs. Confidence** — emotion adds an emotional label to information while confidence adds a label of certainty.

Two diagonals are labeled **abstract** and **concrete**.

### The Four Simple Styles

The two axes place four of the seven modules — the *simple styles*:

| Module | Axis 1 | Axis 2 | Diagonal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Teacher** | Analytical | Emotional | Abstract |
| **Mercy** | Associative | Emotional | Concrete |
| **Server** | Analytical | Confidence | Concrete |
| **Perceiver** | Associative | Confidence | Abstract |

#### Mercy Thought

Mercy thought applies emotional labels to concrete experiences and associates between these experiences. This makes the Mercy person naturally aware of social interaction, because each new situation will bring similar situations to mind along with their emotional labels, leading to a natural sense of emotional appropriateness. The same processing can also be used to gain a feeling for how objects and tools function.

#### Perceiver Thought

Perceiver thought is also associative but applies labels of confidence to abstract facts. This makes the Perceiver person naturally aware of reasonableness, because each new fact will bring similar facts to mind along with their labels of confidence, leading to a natural sense of which facts are reasonable and which are not. Perceiver thought also builds connections between facts, leading to an emphasis upon objects and how they relate.

#### Server Thought

Server thought uses confidence but applies labels of confidence to concrete sequences. This leads to a natural focus upon physical actions, because physical action uses the body to generate concrete sequences of movement. The Server person finds it easy to learn actions and to act in a fluid manner.

#### Teacher Thought

Teacher thought uses emotion but Teacher emotion is quite different than Mercy emotion. Mercy thought adds emotional labels to concrete experiences. Teacher thought, in contrast, **feels good when there is order-within-complexity** — when many items can be summarized by a simple abstract sequence (Friesen, 2021). Teacher thought feels bad when there is an exception to the general rule. Teacher emotion commonly expresses itself as an appreciation of verbal theories, because a verbal theory is a simple abstract sequence that brings order and structure to many specific items. Beauty, elegance, symmetry, order, and structure are all examples of Teacher emotion.

### Interaction Between Emotion and Confidence

Confidence interacts with emotion. For instance, Perceiver thought may have sufficient confidence to know a certain set of facts when studying, while having insufficient confidence when attempting to retrieve these same facts under the emotional stress of an exam. Similarly, Server thought may have sufficient confidence to perform a sequence of actions when practicing a musical piece at home, while having insufficient confidence to perform the same piece when faced with the emotional stress of performing in a concert.

## How Cognitive Modules Cooperate

Cognitive modules do not function in isolation, but rather cooperate to generate thought and behavior.

### Abstract Thought: Teacher and Perceiver

Teacher and Perceiver thought cooperate to generate abstract thought. Perceiver thought comes up with facts and Teacher thought assembles these facts into general theories. Using an analogy, this is like gathering a collection of bricks and then assembling these bricks into a building. The bricks are evaluated using Perceiver confidence, while the structure of the building leads to Teacher emotion.

### Concrete Thought: Mercy and Server

Mercy and Server thought cooperate to generate concrete thought. Mercy thought focuses upon experiences and how they feel, while Server thought carries out the actions that are required to reach desirable experiences and avoid unpleasant experiences. The steps that are required to reach a goal are evaluated using Server confidence, but the goal itself involves Mercy emotions.

### The Role of Perceiver and Server in Concrete Thought

Mercy and Server are not directly connected in the diagram of mental symmetry. Instead, the connections run from Mercy to Perceiver to Contributor to Server. Thus, Perceiver thought organizes Mercy experiences into categories and builds connections between these categories, resulting in a Perceiver map within which Mercy experiences are placed. Contributor thought then chooses which Server action to perform in order to move from one location in the map to another. This means that **Perceiver thought plays a passive mapping role in concrete thought while Server thought plays an active movement role**. Perceiver thought constructs mental maps; Server thought moves within these maps; the goal is either to reach pleasant Mercy experiences or to avoid unpleasant Mercy experiences.

### The Role of Server and Perceiver in Abstract Thought

Similarly, Teacher and Perceiver are not directly connected. Instead, the connections run from Teacher to Server to Contributor to Perceiver. Thus, Server thought chunks the stream of Teacher words into Server sequences of words, sentences, paragraphs, and chapters, leading to the Server structure of syntax. Contributor thought then uses the syntax of Server thought to convey some semantic meaning within Perceiver thought. This means that **Server thought plays the passive mapping role in abstract thought while Perceiver thought plays the active role of generating meaning**. Abstract thought requires a Server 'map' of syntactical structures; abstract thought generates Perceiver meaning. This abstract 'movement' is guided by Teacher feelings of order-within-complexity.

This type of abstract movement can be seen in the examinations of a human doctor. Each examination consists of some context of Server sequences and procedures including examination, diagnosis, and treatment. Perceiver thought is then used between examinations to 'teleport' from one patient to another.

## The Three Composite Styles

The *composite styles* of Exhorter, Contributor, and Facilitator integrate corresponding regions in the two hemispheres:

- **Exhorter** combines Teacher and Mercy
- **Contributor** combines Server and Perceiver
- **Facilitator** facilitates the functioning of the entire mind, emphasizing the relationship between Perceiver and Mercy thought, as well as the relationship between Server and Teacher thought

The three composite styles also provide a chain of processing: Exhorter thought provides motivation and imagination; this is followed by Contributor thought which chooses between various options suggested by Exhorter thought; Facilitator thought then maintains equilibrium by mixing and adjusting within the context of Contributor decision.

### Exhorter Thought

Exhorter thought connects Mercy with Teacher. Mercy thought applies emotional labels to experiential memories, leading among other things to a strong social awareness. Teacher thought comes up with general theories, which are usually communicated using words. Exhorter thought **learns lessons from life**, deriving general theories from personal experiences. Going the other way, Exhorter thought also applies theories to the emotional experiences of life. This relationship between experience and theory can be seen in intuition.

### Contributor Thought

Perceiver thought looks for connections between facts and Server thought focuses upon relating sequences, whereas Contributor thought focuses upon connecting specific Perceiver facts with specific Server sequences. This combination leads in concrete thought to a sense of **cause-and-effect** as Perceiver facts are connected with Server actions, and in abstract thought to **precise meaning** as Perceiver facts are connected with Server sequences of words.

## Three Primary Modes of Integrated Thought

The integrated mind can function in one of three primary ways:

| Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Technical thought** | The entire mind functions in an integrated manner under the control of Exhorter motivation and Contributor thought |
| **Normal thought** | The entire mind is coordinated by the analogies and metaphors of Perceiver and Server thought |
| **Mental networks** | The entire mind is guided by emotional schema within Mercy and/or Teacher thought |

## Mental Networks

A **mental network** is a collection of emotional memories that functions as a unit. Triggering one memory in a mental network will activate the entire mental network, which will then use emotional pressure to impose its content upon the mind. A sufficiently traumatic emotional situation will cause a mental network to form, but a mental network will also form whenever some theory or schema continues to be used for a sufficient length of time. Going the other way, a mental network will generate strong negative emotions if it continues to be triggered and encounters input that is inconsistent with its structure.

Mental networks and their neurological basis are discussed extensively in another paper (Friesen, 2024), which suggests that the mental network provides a unified explanation for the diverse functions of the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC).

### Two Kinds of Mental Network

Two of the seven cognitive modules function emotionally: Mercy thought adds emotional labels to experiences while Teacher thought feels positive emotion when many items fit together in a simple manner. This leads to two kinds of mental network:

**Mercy Mental Networks (MMNs)** are based in collections of emotional experiences. Mental symmetry suggests that the mind uses MMNs to represent people and that most social interaction happens within people's minds as people and situations trigger MMNs that represent those people and situations. For instance, a child has extensive emotional experiences with his or her mother, causing an MMN to form within the child's mind that represents mother and her likes and dislikes. A single glance from mother is sufficient to trigger this MMN, reminding the child of mother's expectations. Similarly, entering some location, such as a bank, triggers a mental network which provides an emotional schema of how one should behave within that location.

**Teacher Mental Networks (TMNs)** emerge when some theory or system of order-within-complexity continues to be used. MMNs develop naturally in the human mind because the physical body imposes emotional experiences upon Mercy thought. Most TMNs emerge through a three-stage process:
1. Mental content is acquired through some form of education.
2. Teacher thought constructs a theory that brings order to this complexity of content.
3. This theory continues to be used long enough to turn into a TMN.

## Technical Thought

Both normal and technical thought can be subdivided into **concrete** and **abstract** components.

### Concrete Technical Thought

The distinction between normal concrete thought and concrete technical thought can be illustrated by comparing normal activity with a game. Normal activity remains somewhat open-ended. For instance, one may decide to perform the Server action of 'going to the store' guided by the Mercy goal of 'getting some groceries', but this is a vague plan that will be adjusted to suit the circumstances.

In a game like chess, all of the parameters are carefully defined. The game occurs on a limited, carefully defined playing field. The pieces are precisely defined in Perceiver thought: bishops, knights, and so on. The Server actions performed by these pieces are also precisely defined: bishops move diagonally, knights move in an 'L shape'. And the goal — capturing the opposing king — is also precisely defined. **Concrete technical thought uses a limited collection of Perceiver facts and Server actions that are known with sufficient certainty to reach some well-defined goal.**

### Abstract Technical Thought

Normal conversation uses semi-rigorous Server sequences of words to convey reasonably precise Perceiver meanings. Math is an example of abstract technical thought which uses well-formed mathematical statements to convey precise meanings.

## Types of Truth

Technical thought requires Perceiver facts that are known with sufficient certainty. There are three sources of such certainty:

### Universal Truth

Universal truth increases Perceiver certainty by looking for repeated connections. Whenever some connection is repeated, then Perceiver thought becomes more confident that this connection describes a legitimate fact. Perceiver thought can assert a fact as long as the level of confidence in that fact exceeds the emotional intensity of the situation. Science tries to investigate facts in an objective manner that prevents Perceiver thought from being overwhelmed by subjective Mercy emotions. This corresponds experimentally to passing some test of statistical significance.

### Emotional 'Truth'

Emotional 'truth' uses emotional pressure to overwhelm Perceiver thought. This emotional pressure can happen from a traumatic event, because the emotional intensity of the event will overwhelm Perceiver thought into viewing the specific connections of that event as 'truth'. For instance, a traumatic incident with a vicious dog will lead to the 'belief' that all dogs always bite. Similarly, the statements of some person with great Mercy status will also be regarded as 'truth'. The terms 'belief' and 'truth' are in quotes here because Perceiver thought is being overwhelmed rather than functioning.

### Absolute Truth

Absolute truth is an intermediate form of truth that packages emotional 'truth' in a way that resembles universal truth. This can be seen in a holy book or a textbook. Perceiver thought is being overwhelmed because the ultimate authority for the authoritative text comes from emotional respect in Mercy thought for the source of the book. However, the words of a book acquire stability because they are written down, printed, and distributed to many people. This gives the mass-printed book the impression of conveying universal truth. In addition, a book separates the Perceiver facts of the author from the Mercy status of the author. Thus, a holy book or textbook may initially be believed because of emotional respect for the source, but studying a holy book or textbook makes it possible for Perceiver thought to develop and gain confidence (McCauley, 2011).

### Summary Table

| Type of Truth | Mechanism | How It Becomes Solid Enough for Technical Thought |
|---|---|---|
| **Universal truth** | Repeated connections increase Perceiver confidence | Passing statistical significance; objective, repeatable observation |
| **Emotional 'truth'** | Emotional pressure overwhelms Perceiver thought | High Mercy status of the source; traumatic emotional intensity |
| **Absolute truth** | Emotional 'truth' packaged to resemble universal truth | Official status of a holy book or textbook |

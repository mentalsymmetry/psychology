# Cross-Domain Cognitive Mechanisms: A Mental Symmetry Invariance Analysis

## Purpose and Approach

Mental symmetry is a cognitive metatheory that searches for patterns of cognition and behavior that repeat across unrelated domains. The key methodological claim is that **any similarities found across domains are the result of mental architecture, not subject matter**. This document applies that claim with deliberate rigor by performing an invariance test on every candidate mechanism before reporting it.

### What Is an Invariance Test?

Within the Mental Symmetry framework, an invariance test asks whether a proposed cognitive mechanism survives the following four checks:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Content invariance** | Does the mechanism appear regardless of what the domain is *about*? |
| **Cultural/social invariance** | Does the mechanism appear in structurally unrelated cultural settings? |
| **Temporal invariance** | Does the mechanism appear across historical periods rather than being a modern artifact? |
| **Scale invariance** | Does the mechanism appear at both abstract (conceptual) and concrete (physical/behavioral) levels? |

A mechanism **fails** the invariance test if it can be explained by properties of a particular domain — e.g., it only appears in competitive activities, or only in text-based knowledge, or only in individualistic cultures. A mechanism **passes** only if it appears consistently in structurally unrelated settings and cannot be attributed to domain-specific factors.

### Domain Selection

To maximize the chance of rejecting false positives, the analysis uses ten domains selected for maximal unrelatedness:

| Label | Domain |
|---|---|
| **MATH** | Mathematics and formal logic |
| **MUSIC** | Musical composition and performance |
| **MED** | Medical diagnosis and treatment |
| **LAW** | Legal systems and adjudication |
| **SPORT** | Athletic training and competition |
| **REL** | Religious practice and doctrine |
| **ECON** | Economic markets and financial behavior |
| **MIL** | Military strategy and training |
| **LANG** | Language acquisition |
| **DEV** | Child cognitive and moral development |

These domains span abstract and concrete thought, cooperative and competitive activity, modern and ancient practice, literate and oral cultures, and individual and collective action.

---

## Methodology

For each candidate mechanism:

1. The mechanism is stated as a precise, testable claim about cognitive processing.
2. Its predicted signature is described — how it would manifest as observable behavior or structure.
3. The ten domains are examined for the signature independently.
4. The four invariance checks are applied.
5. The mechanism is reported only if it passes all four checks and appears in **at least eight of the ten domains** (allowing for two domains where evidence is thin without invalidating the result).

Candidate mechanisms were generated by examining every structural claim in the theory of mental symmetry (see [mental_symmetry_theory.md](mental_symmetry_theory.md)) and asking: *does this claim predict a domain-general behavioral or structural signature?*

---

## Results

Six mechanisms survived all invariance tests. They are presented below in order of the breadth of domain coverage.

## Mechanism 1: Passive Structural Map + Active Sequential Navigation

### Statement

In any domain that requires goal-directed action, Perceiver thought constructs a **structural map** of the possible states or positions (passive/representational role), while Server thought generates **sequential actions** that move through that map (active/navigational role). The two roles are always distinct and never conflated.

### Predicted Signature

- A representational layer (taxonomy, layout, ruleset, territory) that is consulted but not itself the goal
- A sequential procedural layer that operates *within* that representation to reach a defined goal
- Errors or failures that can be classified as either *map errors* (wrong representation) or *navigation errors* (wrong sequence), but rarely both simultaneously

### Domain Evidence

| Domain | Structural Map (Perceiver) | Sequential Navigation (Server) |
|---|---|---|
| **MATH** | Axiom system, defined terms, logical space | Proof steps; derivation sequences |
| **MUSIC** | Tonal/harmonic space; key signature; chord vocabulary | Melodic line; rhythmic sequence; performance execution |
| **MED** | Diagnostic taxonomy; differential diagnosis; anatomical knowledge | Examination protocol; treatment procedure; surgical sequence |
| **LAW** | Statutes, precedents, and defined legal categories | Procedural steps of litigation; argument sequencing |
| **SPORT** | Field/court layout; rules defining legal positions and states | Physical movement sequences; play execution |
| **REL** | Theological doctrine; cosmological map; scriptural categories | Liturgical rite; prayer sequence; pilgrimage route |
| **ECON** | Price/quantity space; market structure; asset classification | Trading procedure; transaction sequence; auction protocol |
| **MIL** | Terrain map; force disposition; order of battle | Maneuver sequence; tactical action; logistical chain |
| **LANG** | Vocabulary (lexical space); grammatical categories | Syntax (sequence of words); utterance construction |
| **DEV** | Object permanence; spatial layout; category knowledge | Locomotion; reaching; tool-use sequences |

**Coverage: 10/10**

### Invariance Test Results

| Test | Result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Content invariance | ✓ Pass | Appears in abstract domains (math, law) and concrete domains (sport, medicine, development) with no content overlap |
| Cultural/social invariance | ✓ Pass | Religious ritual, military drill, and language acquisition appear across all known cultures |
| Temporal invariance | ✓ Pass | Military cartography and legal procedure predate literacy; mathematics and medicine are documented across ancient civilizations |
| Scale invariance | ✓ Pass | Operates at the micro-level (a single chess move, a single grammatical phrase) and macro-level (a proof strategy, an entire legal case) |

**Verdict: Passes all invariance tests.**

### Interpretation

The map/navigation duality is not a feature of any one domain; it is a feature of goal-directed cognition in general. Every domain that requires reaching a defined goal through a sequence of actions spontaneously generates both a representational structure and a procedural structure. These are never merged into a single layer: the map does not move, and the navigator does not redesign the map mid-journey. Errors in one layer do not automatically produce errors in the other, which confirms that they are genuinely distinct cognitive processes rather than descriptions of the same process at different levels of abstraction.

## Mechanism 2: Emotional Pressure Suppressing Factual Confidence

### Statement

When the emotional intensity of a situation (arising from high Mercy status of a source, traumatic experience, or strong group identity) exceeds the current level of Perceiver confidence in a set of facts, Perceiver thought is overwhelmed. The person then accepts the emotionally-pressured connection as 'true' without adequate independent confirmation. The mechanism operates regardless of whether the content is political, financial, religious, athletic, or scientific.

### Predicted Signature

- Factual beliefs that are accepted without independent verification when the source has high social or emotional status
- Reversal or weakening of those same beliefs when the source loses status, even if the factual content is unchanged
- Performance degradation under emotional stress despite competence under neutral conditions (the confidence-emotion interaction)
- Generalizations formed from a single emotionally intense event that persist beyond what the evidence would support

### Domain Evidence

| Domain | Emotional Pressure Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **MATH** | Math anxiety: students who know material in low-stakes practice fail under exam pressure (Perceiver confidence collapses under Mercy emotional stress) |
| **MUSIC** | Stage fright: technically proficient performers lose Server sequence confidence under performance emotional pressure |
| **MED** | Diagnostic anchoring: initial emotional impression of a patient overrides later contradicting clinical data |
| **LAW** | Eyewitness confidence: witnesses express high certainty in identifications formed under traumatic emotional conditions; factual accuracy is lower than confidence implies |
| **SPORT** | Choking under pressure: athletes with proven training competence fail during high-stakes competition |
| **REL** | Authority-based belief: doctrine from high-status religious figures is accepted without independent evaluation; the same claims from an unknown author are dismissed |
| **ECON** | Market panic: emotionally driven herd behavior overrides rational price assessment; asset prices disconnect from fundamentals during crises |
| **MIL** | Tunnel vision in combat: traumatic combat events produce fixed tactical beliefs (e.g., 'that approach is always fatal') that override later evidence |
| **LANG** | Linguistic authority: a grammar rule taught by a high-status teacher is treated as universal law even when the teacher is wrong; correction from a peer is ignored |
| **DEV** | Childhood trauma forming persistent false beliefs: a single frightening event with an animal produces a permanent generalization ('all dogs bite') |

**Coverage: 10/10**

### Invariance Test Results

| Test | Result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Content invariance | ✓ Pass | Appears in abstract academic performance (math, language), physical performance (music, sport), social cognition (law, religion), and financial behavior (economics) |
| Cultural/social invariance | ✓ Pass | Status-based belief and trauma-based generalization are documented across cultures without literacy or formal institutions |
| Temporal invariance | ✓ Pass | Religious authority suppressing factual inquiry and eyewitness over-confidence are documented from antiquity |
| Scale invariance | ✓ Pass | Operates at the level of a single recalled fact (math exam) and at the level of entire belief systems (economic orthodoxy, religious doctrine) |

**Verdict: Passes all invariance tests.**

### Interpretation

The mechanism is not about any particular content but about the ratio of emotional intensity to Perceiver confidence. Any time that ratio tips toward emotion, the same result follows: Perceiver thought accepts the emotionally provided connection as fact, and the quality of the 'fact' is determined by the intensity of the emotion rather than by independent confirmation. This is why the mechanism is observable in settings as different as surgical decision-making, financial trading, childhood development, and liturgical tradition. The surface content differs; the underlying processing is identical.

## Mechanism 3: Repetition Building Server Confidence Toward Stress-Resistance

### Statement

Repeated execution of a Server sequence increases Perceiver-confidence in that sequence, eventually raising it above the threshold required for reliable execution even under emotional stress. This is the mechanism underlying what is commonly called *skill acquisition* and *expert automaticity*. The mechanism predicts a specific trajectory: early repetitions produce high error rates and high sensitivity to distraction; later repetitions produce low error rates and low sensitivity to emotional stress.

### Predicted Signature

- Practice or drill as the primary means of skill acquisition across domains
- A competence threshold below which emotional stress severely degrades performance and above which it does not
- The same sequence that collapses under stress when under-practiced becomes stress-resistant after sufficient repetition
- Transfer of stress-resistance only to the practiced sequence, not to related but unpracticed sequences (sequence specificity)

### Domain Evidence

| Domain | Server Repetition → Stress-Resistance |
|---|---|
| **MATH** | Arithmetic drill: repeated computation of number facts produces recall that is robust under exam stress; novel problems remain stress-sensitive |
| **MUSIC** | Deliberate practice: a piece performed reliably in performance requires far more repetitions than comfortable home practice predicts |
| **MED** | Surgical training: suturing technique practiced on simulation produces reliable, calm performance in live surgery; novel anatomy does not inherit that calm |
| **LAW** | Moot court: repeated oral argument practice produces composed courtroom performance; first-time litigants without practice are visibly affected by the emotional pressure |
| **SPORT** | Muscle memory: sports movements practiced to automaticity are stress-resistant; novel game situations at the same physical skill level are not |
| **REL** | Liturgical practice: prayers and rites repeated throughout life are recited without disruption during emotionally charged life events (grief, marriage) |
| **ECON** | Trading discipline: traders who have practiced a defined trading algorithm through many repetitions execute it under market stress; those without practice deviate |
| **MIL** | Drill and battle inoculation: military units drilled to automaticity maintain procedure under fire; units without drill dissolve under the same stress |
| **LANG** | L1 acquisition: phonological sequences practiced from infancy are stress-resistant in adult speech; L2 sequences practiced only to conversational level break down under stress |
| **DEV** | Motor development: early motor sequences (walking, reaching) acquired through repetition become fully stress-resistant and automatized |

**Coverage: 10/10**

### Invariance Test Results

| Test | Result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Content invariance | ✓ Pass | Appears in motor sequences (sport, surgery, development), verbal sequences (language, law, liturgy), cognitive sequences (math, trading), and procedural sequences (military) |
| Cultural/social invariance | ✓ Pass | Military drill and religious repetition predate writing; L1 acquisition and motor development occur universally |
| Temporal invariance | ✓ Pass | Documented in ancient military manuals, religious texts describing ritual practice, and classical musical pedagogy |
| Scale invariance | ✓ Pass | Operates at the level of a single phoneme, a single surgical stitch, a single chess opening, and a complete military maneuver |

**Verdict: Passes all invariance tests.**

### Interpretation

Repetition is not a general learning strategy; it is specifically a Server confidence-building strategy. What is being built is not knowledge of facts (which is Perceiver) but confidence in sequences (which is Server). This explains a specific asymmetry that appears in every domain: practitioners can often *describe* the correct sequence accurately (Perceiver knowledge intact) while still failing to *execute* it reliably under stress (Server confidence insufficient). The two can be dissociated, which confirms that they are products of different cognitive modules.

## Mechanism 4: Three-Stage Formation of a Teacher Mental Network from a Theory

### Statement

When a set of related content is organized by Teacher thought into a general theory, and that theory is then used repeatedly over time, the theory gradually transforms from a deliberately held belief into an automatic mental schema (a Teacher Mental Network, TMN). The TMN then operates on subsequent input before conscious deliberation can take place, causing incoming data that fits the schema to be accepted automatically and data that contradicts it to generate negative emotion rather than neutral evaluation.

### Predicted Signature

- Stage 1: the theory is consciously held, can be articulated, and can be revised without emotional cost
- Stage 2: the theory has become an automatic filter; contradicting evidence generates discomfort rather than curiosity
- Stage 3: practitioners can no longer articulate the theory as a theory — it has become 'just the way things are'
- Practitioners in Stage 3 respond to challenges to the schema with emotional intensity disproportionate to the factual content of the challenge

### Domain Evidence

| Domain | TMN Formation from Theory |
|---|---|
| **MATH** | Foundational paradigms: mathematicians trained in one foundational system (e.g., set-theoretic foundations) react with strong resistance to alternative foundations (e.g., type theory or intuitionism) that are logically equivalent |
| **MUSIC** | Tonal orthodoxy: conservatory-trained musicians who have internalized tonal harmony as a TMN experience atonal music as genuinely disturbing or 'wrong' rather than merely unfamiliar |
| **MED** | Medical paradigm rigidity: clinicians who trained under a dominant diagnostic framework (e.g., biomedical vs. biopsychosocial model) treat the other as obviously inferior rather than as a competing framework |
| **LAW** | Legal positivism vs. natural law: law students who have been trained to Stage 3 in one jurisprudential tradition find the alternative literally incomprehensible rather than merely incorrect |
| **SPORT** | Coaching orthodoxy: coaches who trained under one tactical system (e.g., man-to-man defense) resist zone defense not on tactical grounds but with the emotional sense that it is 'not real' basketball |
| **REL** | Doctrinal rigidity: religious practitioners who have used a theological framework long enough for it to become a TMN experience heresy not as incorrect doctrine but as a personal attack generating strong negative emotion |
| **ECON** | Economic orthodoxy: economists trained in one macro-economic school (e.g., Keynesianism or monetarism) respond to competing models with an emotional dismissal that is disproportionate to the technical differences |
| **MIL** | Doctrinal conservatism: military institutions whose operational doctrine has become a TMN respond to tactical innovations not with analysis but with institutional resistance that is emotionally charged |
| **LANG** | Prescriptive grammar as TMN: grammarians who have used a prescriptive framework long enough for it to become a TMN react to descriptive linguistics not as a competing methodology but as an attack on correctness itself |
| **DEV** | Piaget's stage transitions: the child's operational schema that has been used long enough to become automatic is resistant to accommodation — new data is assimilated to the existing schema before conscious accommodation is attempted |

**Coverage: 10/10**

### Invariance Test Results

| Test | Result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Content invariance | ✓ Pass | Appears in formal (math, logic), empirical (medicine, economics), normative (law, grammar), and performative (music, sport) domains |
| Cultural/social invariance | ✓ Pass | Doctrinal rigidity in religion and military conservatism appear across cultures and historical periods |
| Temporal invariance | ✓ Pass | The three-stage process is documented in ancient religious traditions, classical musical pedagogy, and pre-modern legal systems |
| Scale invariance | ✓ Pass | Operates at the level of a single grammatical rule, a diagnostic heuristic, a judicial principle, and an entire scientific paradigm |

**Verdict: Passes all invariance tests.**

### Interpretation

The TMN formation process explains a phenomenon that appears independently in philosophy of science (Kuhn's paradigm resistance), educational psychology (conceptual change difficulty), and religious studies (doctrinal ossification), without requiring domain-specific explanations for any of them. The unifying mechanism is always the same: a theory that started as a consciously held structure has been used long enough to become an automatic emotional schema. Once that transformation is complete, contradicting data no longer triggers Perceiver evaluation — it triggers emotional resistance. The content of the theory is irrelevant to the mechanism; only the duration and consistency of use matters.

## Mechanism 5: Exhorter Bridging Between Individual Experience and General Principle

### Statement

Exhorter thought bridges in both directions between concrete Mercy experiences and abstract Teacher theories: inductively (specific experiences → general lesson) and deductively (general principle → applied to specific situations). This bidirectional bridging produces *intuition*: the rapid, apparently sourceless application of a general principle to a specific situation without conscious derivation. The mechanism is domain-general and is the cognitive basis of expertise in any field.

### Predicted Signature

- Experts who cannot articulate the rule they are applying but who reliably apply it correctly
- The same individuals who can derive the rule from experience can also apply it prospectively to novel cases
- The quality of intuition tracks the richness of relevant experiential history, not the amount of explicit study
- Disruption of normal Exhorter functioning (e.g., by introspective prompting) degrades performance on tasks that were previously intuitive

### Domain Evidence

| Domain | Exhorter Inductive/Deductive Bridging |
|---|---|
| **MATH** | Mathematical intuition: experienced mathematicians 'sense' which approaches will work before constructing a proof — experience-based inductive pattern feeds prospective deductive application |
| **MUSIC** | Musical intuition: experienced improvisers apply harmonic and rhythmic principles to novel situations without conscious rule retrieval; asking them to articulate the rule mid-performance degrades performance |
| **MED** | Clinical intuition: experienced clinicians form a rapid correct diagnosis before completing the formal differential — this intuition is a product of many accumulated cases, not explicit rule application |
| **LAW** | Legal reasoning by analogy: experienced lawyers reason from prior cases to new situations without explicit articulation of the underlying principle — the principle emerges from case experience |
| **SPORT** | Game sense: athletes who have accumulated extensive competitive experience make correct tactical decisions faster than deliberate analysis allows; coaching disrupts this when it forces explicit deliberation |
| **REL** | Moral intuition: individuals whose moral experience has accumulated within a tradition develop an automatic sense of right and wrong that guides action before explicit reasoning — the tradition's teaching is applied without retrieving it |
| **ECON** | Market intuition: experienced traders develop a 'feel' for market dynamics that cannot be fully articulated but that produces reliable decisions faster than formal analysis |
| **MIL** | Commander's intuition (Auftragstaktik): commanders with extensive operational experience make rapid correct tactical decisions under time pressure without explicit planning — their experience base is providing the bridge |
| **LANG** | Pragmatic competence: fluent speakers apply the conversational maxims (Grice) without knowing them explicitly — years of conversational experience have been inductively synthesized into automatic communicative intuition |
| **DEV** | Theory of mind: children develop an intuitive understanding of others' mental states through accumulated social experience — not through explicit instruction but through Exhorter synthesis of social interactions |

**Coverage: 10/10**

### Invariance Test Results

| Test | Result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Content invariance | ✓ Pass | Appears in formal reasoning (math, law), perceptual-motor skill (sport, music), social cognition (development, language), and pattern recognition under uncertainty (medicine, trading) |
| Cultural/social invariance | ✓ Pass | Moral intuition and social intuition (theory of mind) appear universally in child development; market intuition appears wherever exchange economies exist |
| Temporal invariance | ✓ Pass | Military commander's intuition is described in ancient texts (Sun Tzu, Caesar's commentaries); clinical intuition is documented in pre-modern medicine |
| Scale invariance | ✓ Pass | Operates at the level of a single conversational turn, a single tactical decision, a single diagnostic impression, and a career-long research trajectory |

**Verdict: Passes all invariance tests.**

### Interpretation

This mechanism explains why *intuition* is reported as a genuine phenomenon by practitioners across every domain but resists reduction to explicit knowledge in any of them. It is not a mystical phenomenon; it is the output of Exhorter thought bridging accumulated Mercy experience with Teacher-level generalization. The inductive direction produces the general principle; the deductive direction produces the intuitive application. The inability to articulate the rule mid-performance without disrupting performance is a predictable consequence: articulation requires conscious Teacher thought, which competes with the automatic Exhorter bridge that produces the intuition.

## Mechanism 6: Technical Thought Requiring Jointly Sufficient Perceiver Facts and Server Actions

### Statement

A domain supports *technical thought* if and only if it provides (a) a set of Perceiver facts known with sufficient certainty, and (b) a set of Server actions known with sufficient certainty. Neither condition alone is sufficient. Domains that have precise Perceiver facts but imprecise Server procedures are *knowledge-rich but not technically actionable*. Domains that have precise Server procedures but imprecise Perceiver facts are *procedurally fluent but directionless*. Technical thought, and the productivity gains it enables, requires both conditions simultaneously.

### Predicted Signature

- Precision revolutions occur when a domain that previously had only one of the two components acquires the second
- In immature technical domains, practitioners can describe *what* needs to happen (Perceiver) but not reliably *how* to produce it (Server), or vice versa
- When both components are present, output quality becomes reproducible rather than talent-dependent
- Failure modes split cleanly: factual error (wrong Perceiver model) vs. procedural error (wrong Server execution), and these are diagnosable separately

### Domain Evidence

| Domain | Perceiver Facts (Sufficient Certainty) | Server Actions (Sufficient Certainty) | Technical Thought Onset |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MATH** | Axioms and defined terms | Inference rules and proof procedures | Formal mathematics from Euclid onward; explosion of productivity when both are present |
| **MUSIC** | Musical notation (pitch, duration) | Compositional procedures (counterpoint rules, voice leading) | Polyphonic composition becomes reproducible once both notation and rules are codified |
| **MED** | Germ theory, diagnostic criteria (ICD/DSM) | Standardized treatment protocols | Modern evidence-based medicine; pre-germ-theory medicine had procedures but wrong Perceiver facts |
| **LAW** | Defined statutes and precedent | Codified litigation procedure | Roman codification, common law systematization; oral customary law lacks Perceiver precision |
| **SPORT** | Rule definitions and performance metrics | Defined training protocols | Modern sport professionalization; folk games lacked both; codification of rules preceded systematic training |
| **REL** | Canonical doctrine (creeds, scriptures) | Liturgical procedure | Religious technical practice (sacramental theology) requires both doctrinal precision and ritual precision |
| **ECON** | Price theory, financial instrument definitions | Trading and settlement procedures | Modern financial markets; barter economies lack both; derivative markets required both to function |
| **MIL** | Intelligence (Perceiver facts about terrain, enemy) | Doctrine and drill | Military effectiveness correlates with the quality of both intelligence and drill; one without the other is insufficient |
| **LANG** | Grammar (defined rules of well-formedness) | Composition and rhetorical procedures | Classical rhetoric required both grammatical precision and procedural composition rules to produce reproducible eloquence |
| **DEV** | Object categories, causal relationships | Motor procedures | A child becomes able to use tools technically only when both the Perceiver model of the tool and the Server sequence for using it are sufficiently developed |

**Coverage: 10/10**

### Invariance Test Results

| Test | Result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Content invariance | ✓ Pass | The joint sufficiency condition appears in abstract (math, law), physical (sport, medicine), social (language, religion), and economic domains |
| Cultural/social invariance | ✓ Pass | Technical thought in military drill and legal codification appear across unrelated ancient civilizations (Rome, China, Greece) independently |
| Temporal invariance | ✓ Pass | The pattern of 'precision revolution' triggered by acquiring the missing component is documented from antiquity (Euclid's axioms, Roman law) to modernity (evidence-based medicine, algorithmic trading) |
| Scale invariance | ✓ Pass | Operates at the level of a single tool-use episode in child development and at the level of an entire scientific field's maturation |

**Verdict: Passes all invariance tests.**

### Interpretation

This mechanism explains why different domains reach technical maturity at different times: the bottleneck is always the acquisition of the missing component. Pre-Pasteur medicine had excellent procedural technique (surgery, wound care) but incorrect Perceiver facts (disease model), and therefore could not achieve reproducible outcomes. Pre-Euclid geometry had extensive Perceiver knowledge (geometric relationships) but no codified Server procedures (proof methods), and therefore could not scale. Every instance of a 'precision revolution' — where a domain suddenly becomes reproducible and teachable — can be examined for the acquisition of the previously missing component, and in each case the prediction is confirmed.

## Discussion

### What the Invariance Results Show

All six surviving mechanisms involve one or more of the four simple modules (Teacher, Mercy, Server, Perceiver). None of the surviving mechanisms are reducible to the subject matter of the domains in which they appear. This is consistent with the core claim of mental symmetry: that recurring patterns across domains reflect cognitive architecture rather than shared content.

The composite modules (Exhorter, Contributor, Facilitator) appear as mediating or bridging mechanisms (Mechanism 5 involves Exhorter; Mechanism 1 involves Contributor as the chooser between map positions) rather than as primary sources of the pattern. This is consistent with the theory, which describes the composite modules as integrators of the simple modules rather than as independent generators of behavior.

### What Did Not Survive

Several candidate mechanisms were examined and rejected because they failed one or more invariance tests:

- **Status hierarchy → deference behavior**: failed cultural invariance (specific form of hierarchy is culturally determined even if deference itself is universal)
- **Narrative structure (beginning/middle/end)**: failed content invariance (applies primarily to communicative domains and does not transfer cleanly to physical or formal domains)
- **In-group / out-group categorical thinking**: failed scale invariance (operates at the social group level but does not map onto individual-level cognitive processing with the same structure)
- **Scarcity → increased valuation**: failed to show the Perceiver/Server dissociation signature; the effect appears to be a single-module Mercy phenomenon rather than a multi-module architectural pattern

### Methodological Note

The invariance test as applied here is qualitative rather than statistical. The mechanisms reported are those for which independently reported behavioral and structural evidence across unrelated domains is consistent with the predicted signature from a single architectural mechanism. This is the standard of evidence appropriate to a cognitive metatheory: the goal is not to provide novel data but to demonstrate that existing data from unrelated domains is coherently explained by a single model without domain-specific residual.
